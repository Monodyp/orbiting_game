# Ice Ice Water! Architecture

## FPS pivot — 2026-09-14
`implementation_plan.md` was approved, with mobile support explicitly retained. Freeze-tag architecture is archived in `docs/archive/freeze-tag-architecture.md`. No new major runtime dependency is needed.

## Boundaries and stack
- `apps/client`: TypeScript, React HUD/lobby/settings, Three.js, Vite, pointer-lock/touch input, prediction, remote interpolation, Web Audio.
- `apps/server`: Node.js, Colyseus, Express invite/session APIs, validated fixed-step movement, authoritative combat/lifecycle, PostgreSQL repositories.
- `packages/shared`: protocol, configurable constants, pure map/collision/movement functions. No authority decisions, secrets, database, or rendering.
- One room belongs to one process. Live state stays in memory. No Redis, physics engine, alternate app structure, or client/server cross-imports.

## Simulation and combat
The server runs at 20 Hz. Sequenced movement is bounded and queued; message volume cannot purchase time. Server and prediction share 50 ms movement including velocity/friction, air control, jump, slide deadlines, crouch, sprint, surface modifiers, and deterministic water buoyancy/drag. Swimmers float partially submerged, can hold jump to rise, and cannot gain sprint or slide boosts; Original World additionally treats crouch as a bounded dive input while buoyancy remains authoritative. Static collision uses substeps against map-specific shared geometry. Frostline uses analytic blocks/ramps. Frost Island uses hybrid collision: transformed centimetre-quantized Fort triangles in immutable three-metre X/Z cells plus analytic boxes for procedural islands, bridges, ice paths, facilities, and cover. The same authored layout drives rendering, authoritative movement, prediction, grounding, and hitscan. Height queries are bounded by current feet height so players can walk beneath roofs; body clearance and ceiling rays prevent walking/jumping through walls and ceilings. Players do not block one another.

There is no weapon or hitscan state. Ice and Water send an empty `action/lunge` intent to begin a server-owned 600ms lunge at twice running speed with a 3-second cooldown; the server captures the most recent validated world-space movement intent at activation, normalizes it, and falls back to authoritative facing when idle. The captured vector remains fixed until the lunge expires, when all lunge momentum is cleared. Repeated active/cooldown requests are ignored without an error. Airborne movement automatically enters a wall-run when a movement direction presses into a nearby shared collision wall; gravity is reduced during contact and jump applies an outward kick. Left-click sends an empty `action/interact` intent; the server selects the nearest eligible player from authoritative positions and accepts only a three-dimensional distance of at most `GAMEPLAY.interactionRange` (`2m`). Ice interacting with alive Water freezes them and applies an authoritative roughly 2-metre backward/upward impulse; Water interacting with a frozen Water teammate rescues them immediately. Frozen movement is rejected after the knockback arc, rescue protection is server-owned, and the match finishes immediately with an Ice win only when every original Water player is frozen.

Spawns score enemy distance and visibility across 16 points per map. Frost Island spawns are authored on the north, west, east, south, southwest, and southeast outer islands, never in the central hotspot; each has a clear initial exit toward combat. Island grounding samples the player's footprint to prevent sinking into adjacent ledges, and standing under low ceilings remains crouched until clear. Ice Ice Water has no health, damage, death, or respawn system; disconnected players cannot act. Input queues and speculative movement clear on disconnect.

## Lifecycle and protocol
Lobby → countdown → playing → finished → intermission → closed. Steps at/after the match deadline are rejected; earlier steps resolve before results. At countdown completion the server shuffles connected humans and development bots, assigns `max(1, round(playerCount / 5))` Ice players, and assigns all remaining players to Water. The match stores its original Water count once at start and derives the authoritative unfrozen count from Water player status. Ice wins immediately only when every original Water player is frozen. At the deadline, Water wins when the current unfrozen count is at least `ceil(originalWaterCount × 0.60)`; otherwise Ice wins. During playing, Ice proximity interactions move an alive Water player into the temporary frozen state; frozen bodies accept no movement. Water proximity interactions immediately rescue a frozen Water teammate. The interaction range, status transition, and brief post-rescue protection are server-owned. Results persist once under a UUID and release invite/memberships once after an eight-second display.

Client messages: `input/move`, `action/interact`, `action/lunge`, `room/start`, `room/configure`, `session/ping`. Events: `player/respawned`, `player/frozen`, `player/rescued`, `match/phase-changed`, `match/result`, `session/pong`, `session/error`. Schema patches synchronize motion/stance, status, rescue progress, team/mode/map, server clock, and the server-derived Water started/unfrozen counters. Deploy client and server together.

React does not drive per-frame movement. Pointer-lock loss/blur neutralizes intent; mobile tracks independent pointers for concurrent look/movement and interaction. Settings are validated local preferences.

## Lobby presentation

The pre-match client keeps one `LobbyScene` alive while React changes between main, play, game-mode, customization, party, profile, and settings panels. The scene owns its Three.js renderer, cinematic camera poses, loaded character instance, procedural frozen-facility environment, and low-cost effects. React communicates state changes through imperative setters; it never drives the render loop.

Music is application-scoped rather than panel- or match-scoped. One Web Audio graph decodes and continuously loops the repository-authored track across the lobby, active match, results, and subsequent matches; it shares persisted master/music/SFX/mute preferences and resumes after a valid interaction when autoplay is blocked. Only application teardown stops the source. Private-room creation remains the Play action; the lobby does not simulate a public matchmaking queue, friends service, progression, rank, currency, or cosmetic inventory.

Frostline weather is client presentation keyed only to authoritative remaining time. A deterministic storm sampler feeds one layered GPU point draw call, a camera-local ground-drift shader, presentation-only structure caps, cloud speed/color, linear fog, light/exposure, peripheral HUD frost, restrained camera sway, and four smoothly mixed procedural Web Audio layers. The effect begins at 4:00 remaining and never mutates the deadline, movement, collision, rules, or protocol. Touch and reduced-effects modes lower the fixed particle allocation; no distant player owns a separate storm simulation.

## Security and access
Retain HMAC-SHA256 signed guests, timing-safe signature verification, per-tab storage, random eight-character invites, origins, 4 KiB payload cap, private/unlisted rooms. Public Colyseus matchmaking remains blocked; authenticated HTTP issues seats and WebSocket auth verifies sessions again. Never log tokens, connection strings, or unnecessary personal data.

Routes remain `/health`, `/ready`, `POST /api/guest-session`, `POST /api/rooms`, `POST /api/rooms/join`. Connected host configures the map in lobby; Ice Ice Water is the fixed, sole game mode. Host start is validated and rate-limited; team assignment has no client message and remains server-owned. Every mutation checks unexpired sessions. Movement/actions have separate budgets plus transport cap. Unknown/legacy messages are rejected.

Population is configurable 1–150, default 150. Dev bots reserve seats, leave one human seat, cannot host, and are disabled in production. Start minimum is one for practice. Ice Ice Water assigns the nearest whole 1 Ice : 4 Water split at countdown completion. Fresh joins close at countdown. Reconnect defaults to 25 seconds and preserves state; expiry advances deadlines then marks spectator and releases membership.

HTTP limits remain 600 guest/room/upgrade requests per minute per direct peer IP, room APIs 10/minute per guest. Forwarded headers are untrusted. HTTPS/WSS requires a trusted proxy with private raw server port outside development.

## Persistence and deployment
Additive `003-arena-matches.sql` creates FPS tables without modifying checksummed historical migrations or deleting old results. `004-arena-map.sql` adds map identity with a Frostline default for previous rows. UUID match/player IDs, UTC dates, mode/map, winner/reason, kills/deaths, team/status are stored atomically and idempotently. Dev bots also get UUIDs. Positions, names, tokens, live timers are excluded. Write failure logs only match ID and does not alter lifecycle.

Node 24.18.0 / npm 11.17.0 and lockfile pins remain. Migrations use transactions/advisory locks/checksums. Local Compose and readiness semantics remain. Free-tier hosting is an unselected measured spike. No deployment is implied. Throughput, mobile performance, and latency fairness remain gates.

## Alternatives and consequences
Replacing freeze-tag avoids maintaining conflicting rule paths; a second mode would double state/HUD/validation complexity and is out of scope. Original weapons, facility geometry, and synthesized combat audio remain. The user-authorized local Island asset is integrated with recorded embedded metadata and an explicit underlying-permission release gate. The supplied wooden-house source and attribution are retained but, as of 2026-09-25, the model and its collider are no longer part of Frostline. Shared kinematics/analytic collision retain the dependency footprint; complex physics and rewind are deferred. Frostline initially replaced the legacy procedural world. The 2026-09-15 user request restores its first authored map as Original World using current FPS rules. The user also approved integrating the supplied Island GLB; `MAP_SPEC.md` defines all three FPS maps.

## Imported map build and capacity evidence

The preserved Fort derivative is 4,937,100 bytes / 61,396 triangles / one material draw call. Frost Island applies `ISLAND_FORT_SCALE` and `ISLAND_FORT_Y` identically while decoding shared collision and positioning the rendered GLB; browser-computed bounds never define gameplay. `island-layout.ts` adds six separate low-detail land masses, multi-route bridge/ice connections, specialized cover, a central Cryogenic Core, an 80-metre half-extent, and outer spawns. Repeated surfaces and cover use instancing grouped by region for frustum culling; the core uses distance-based LOD. Runtime movement remains gated on Fort loading, and resources are disposed on exit. The original 136,646,100-byte source referenced by `assets/island-build-report.json` is currently absent, so `npm run assets:island` cannot rebuild the derivative until that source is restored. Frostline no longer loads the wooden-house GLB or includes its former conservative box in shared collision.

Loading the complete source archipelago would retain thousands of draw calls and oversize geometry, unsuitable for mobile. The selected hybrid preserves the recognizable Fort while making the outer archipelago original, deterministic, lightweight, and shared with server collision. A separate physics dependency remains unnecessary: shared kinematic water forces keep server authority and prediction identical. Exact Fort-triangle plus analytic-layout hitscan and sampled body clearance are explicit compromises; routes, sightlines, swimming, spawn pressure, and physical-device performance need playtesting. Frost Island water has a Y=0 surface, buoyant player movement, and visible perimeter markers.

Measured 150-client initial snapshots exceeded the previous 32 KiB Colyseus encoder buffer; it is now 64 KiB. A rerun completed without buffer overflow. Same-process five-second load checks reached 20/50/100/150 connections, but did not sustain 20 Hz at larger populations. They establish lifecycle smoke coverage, not supported production capacity. See TASKS.md for measurements.

## Original World restoration ? 2026-09-15

The user requested restoring the very first map from their GitHub repository as an additional choice. The first complete map commit is [87a8893](https://github.com/ChadBojelador/orbiting_game/commit/87a8893f6b84acc39f9b2df709153abe26079cdd). Its authored topology and procedural renderer are restored in isolated shared/client modules; no freeze-tag rules, shrinking boundary, or archived protocol are reactivated. The fixed FPS boundary is ?125 m to match the original authored envelope.

An immutable three-metre triangle index baked from the restored renderer supplies terrain, bridge, landmark, vegetation, river-surface, and cover collision to movement, prediction, grounding, ceiling checks, spawn visibility, and hitscan. Regenerate with npm run assets:original; independent Three.js raycasts detect stale geometry. River tops retain their authored walkable elevation; transparent top groups share closed geometry with opaque bank/bottom groups that extend into local terrain. A shallow closed bed behind the transparent surface prevents steep channel transitions from exposing the culled interior of the water volume. The bed is widened and endpoint-extended to overlap every terrain-grid cell removed for the river plus the player radius. Water and bed top groups enter collision to close fall-through gaps; their overlapping support skirts remain presentation so they cannot become invisible barriers. The closed ocean render volume, route paint, boundary, and waterfalls are presentation, while `WATER_BOTTOM` supplies the matching analytic ocean floor. Original World's three crystal landmarks use tip-weighted emissive materials plus broad shadowless regional point lights (85/110/75 metres at Village/Crystal Valley/moonstone). The 2026-09-20 visual polish adds a bounded village light, a broad 90-metre shadowless windmill beacon, hemisphere fill and one player-centered 96-metre moon shadow volume (2048 desktop / 1024 touch / disabled with reduced effects). ACES tone mapping applies to Original World; exponential distance fog is used above water, while underwater fog remains unchanged. OriginalWorldMaterials extends the existing Three.js material chunks with procedural grain, wetness, snow, water normals and a 128-square coastal depth-color texture, avoiding transmission render passes or a new postprocessing stack. OriginalWorldAtmosphere owns instanced ankle-height accents and fewer than 800 GPU-animated points, with distance culling, half density on touch and no particles under reduced effects. Its explicitly marked presentation subtree is excluded from collision baking; a byte-for-byte geometry test guards the existing bake. The windmill tower, integrated beacon section and roof remain baked collision, while the elevated fan is explicitly presentation-only and rotates client-side so it never diverges from a static server collider. Original ocean swimming uses Y=1.5 and an authoritative crouch-to-dive target; camera submersion independently drives short blue-green fog and an audio low-pass filter. Existing Island water behavior is unchanged. Sixteen validated village/inner-approach spawns retain dynamic enemy/visibility/death scoring. Migration 005 extends the map constraint without changing checksummed historical migrations. No dependency or process boundary changes. Original grades/routes, population density, and mobile rendering performance need playtesting.
